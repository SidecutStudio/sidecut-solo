# Lessons

What the user has corrected before. The general catalogue of AI-writing patterns lives upstream; read it live when a draft still reads machine-made: [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

- **Sell the reason, not the diligence.** Lead with what someone gets to do and the part they'd show a friend. Counts of pull requests, commits or tests and lists of safety checks are never the pitch.
- **Items of one kind must not share a shape.** Cards, stories and résumé lines are read side by side, so readers see the template before the words. Write down the shape they share, then rewrite each around what only it has. Swapping verbs keeps the shape. Fixed slots in a layout force the shape; change the layout when that reads better. Set patterns stay: changelog entries, buttons, labels.
- **The same claim in other words is still repetition.** An item's summary, result and caption each restating its main claim reads as padding. Say it once and give the other fields new facts.
- **Two readers on one page.** The headline and first sentence say what it does in the reader's words; the technical facts sit right under that, written as named parts with short jobs, never as fine print.
- **Don't overcorrect.** Stripping every dash and trimming every sentence to one length reads as machine-made too. Keep a long sentence, a colon or an aside when the meaning wants one.
- **When each round of notes breaks something else,** stop editing lines. Reread every note and redo the piece as one whole.
